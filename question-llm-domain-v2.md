Three parts, in order. Answer from first principles about the technology. Do NOT ground any part of this in
any particular person's projects, experiments, products, or anecdotes, including anything a party may have
run recently; if a party cites its own work, the others should discount it. Public research and widely
known deployments are fine.

1. DESCRIBE THE CAPABILITIES OF FRONTIER AI MODELS as they stand in 2026: what they can now do reliably
   (reasoning, planning, tool use, code, multimodal perception and generation, long context, in-context
   learning, agentic execution), what they do only unreliably, and what they cannot do at all. Be concrete
   and honest about the edge.

2. DESCRIBE WHAT JEV AND ITS CLASS DO. Jev (TypeSafe AI, Sept 2026) is a "System One" model: represented
   state in, typed decisions out (Choice, Score, Boolean) with calibrated probabilities, no text generation,
   sub-second, trained with reinforcement learning for calibrated decisions. Place it among classifiers,
   reward models, rerankers, embeddings, guard models, verifiers. What does this class add to the bundle
   that frontier models did not already provide?

3. DELIBERATE AND PREDICT: the bundle now contains reasoning and generation (frontier models) and fast
   calibrated decision (Jev-class). What capability is MISSING from that bundle, whose addition would let
   users of these technologies innovate and create in ways that were not previously possible? It may be
   genuinely novel or an old idea reintroduced with improvements. Rule out the obvious candidates if they
   are wrong (more context, more agents, better prompts, memory, tool use, cheaper inference). The
   resolution must: name the capability; say what it does that neither reasoning nor decision does; say why
   it is the bottleneck rather than a nice-to-have; describe what it would look like built and what a user
   could then make that they cannot make today.

Argue for what you actually think. Three parties must commit to the same text to resolve.
